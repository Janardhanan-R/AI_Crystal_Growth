## 2. Input design (T1–T5)

The five heater set points are not continuously sampled; they take a small discrete set of
levels each: `T1 ∈ {1255, 1275}` (2), `T2 ∈ {1215, 1235, 1255}` (3), `T3 ∈ {1195, 1215, 1235}`
(3), `T4 ∈ {1055, 1075, 1095}` (3), `T5 ∈ {1035, 1055}` (2). The full factorial would give
2×3×3×3×2 = 108 combinations; 96 remain after removing the 12 that violate the physical
ordering constraint `T1 ≥ T2 ≥ T3 ≥ T4 ≥ T5` (0 violations present in the data — the
constraint is fully honored). Pairwise input correlations are ≈0 except `T2`–`T3`
(r = 0.231), confirming this is a constrained factorial DOE, not i.i.d.-sampled inputs.

## 5. Correlations

**Input–output (Pearson):** `T2` and `T3` are the dominant single drivers of `Lc`/`Le`
(r ≈ −0.63 to −0.67), `T4` is a secondary driver (r ≈ −0.50 to −0.51), and `T1`/`T5` are weak
(|r| ≈ 0.07–0.12). This matches the SHAP-based feature-importance ranking already established
earlier in this project (T2, T4 dominate) and is now confirmed independently by a raw Pearson
correlation computed directly on the dataset, without any model in the loop.

**Output–output:** `Lc`–`Le` are extremely collinear (r = 0.992); `Lc`–`P` and `Le`–`P` are
even more so by construction (r = 0.998, since `P` is their average). `D`, being `Lc − Le`,
correlates asymmetrically with its two parents (`Lc`: r = 0.692, `Le`: r = 0.595) — a real,
data-driven signature of a difference variable between two near-collinear quantities, and the
reason `D` is numerically harder to predict than `Lc` or `Le` individually (subtracting two
correlated quantities discards most of their shared signal and amplifies relative noise).

## 6. Derived-variable identities (verified, not assumed)

- `D = Lc − Le` — max abs error 2.73×10⁻¹⁷ across all 96 rows (floating-point exact).

Both are re-verified programmatically here (independently of the earlier project-wide

## 7. Physically-impossible-value screen

No `T1..T5` values ≤ 0 K. No values outside plausible furnace operating ranges (1035–1275 K
throughout, well within realistic VB furnace set points for this process). `D` negative in
39/96 rows: **not** flagged as impossible — `D` is a signed deflection, and forcing it
non-negative would misrepresent the physics (see Data Dictionary). No other physically
impossible values identified. No rows were removed or altered as a result of this screen.

## 8. What this means for synthetic generation

1. **Preserve the factorial input structure**, not just marginal ranges — a synthetic
   generator must respect the near-zero `T1..T5` cross-correlations (except `T2`-`T3`) and,
   critically, the `T1≥T2≥...≥T5` ordering constraint on every generated row.
2. **`D` needs an explicit higher-uncertainty treatment.** It is the most skewed/leptokurtic
   output, the weakest-modeled target in this project's own validated surrogate, and a
   difference of two near-collinear quantities — any synthetic `D` values must carry wider,
   explicitly-stated uncertainty than synthetic `Lc`/`Le`.
3. **`P` stays excluded from generation as an independent target** and is instead computed
   algebraically from generated `Lc`/`Le`, exactly as in the original data, to avoid
   introducing an inconsistent derived column.
4. **n = 96 is small.** Any generation method must be selected and limited accordingly — see
   `02_methodology_selection.md`.
